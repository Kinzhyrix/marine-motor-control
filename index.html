02Actual panel wiring layout

How the same circuit looks inside a typical starter panel or MCC bucket. Power cables are colour-coded per IEC (L1 brown, L2 black, L3 grey). Control wires run inside the wire duct, so you trace them by the ferrule numbers (red tags) on both ends, not by following the wire. Every wire with the same number is the same electrical point.

04Sequence of operation

  1. Q1 closed → 440 V at KM1 line terminals and at T1 via F3/F4. T1 gives 110 V; through F5 this becomes wire 2.
  2. Wire 2 → F2 95-96 (closed) → wire 3 → E-stop S0 (closed) → wire 4 → STOP S1 (closed) → wire 5. KM1 31-32 is closed, so H2 STOPPED is lit.
  3. Press START S2: 5 connects to 6, so KM1 coil A1-A2 is energised. The main contacts close and the motor starts. At the same time KM1 13-14 closes across START.
  4. Release START: the coil stays energised through 13-14. This is the seal-in / holding / latching contact. H1 RUNNING on, H2 off, AMS gets the running signal via 43-44.
  1. Press STOP (or remote E-stop): the series circuit opens, the coil drops, 13-14 opens. Releasing STOP does not restart: the seal is gone.
  2. Overload: F2 heaters bend the bimetal → 95-96 opens (coil drops), 97-98 closes (H3 TRIP). Manual reset only after the cause is found.
  3. Blackout / low voltage: the coil can’t hold, so the contactor drops out. This gives no-volt (undervoltage) release automatically; the motor will not restart on its own when power returns. On ships, essential pumps are restarted deliberately by a sequential-restart system (see section 8).

05The components and what each one does

Q1
MCCB / motor protection breaker

Isolates the starter and clears short circuits. Its breaking capacity must suit the prospective fault level at the switchboard. Ship generators running in parallel give high fault currents. Lockable OFF for isolation.

KM1
Magnetic contactor

An electromagnet (coil A1-A2) pulls in 3 main poles and the auxiliary contacts. Choose it by AC-3 rating (squirrel-cage motor start/stop) at 440 V, not just the amps on the label. Coil voltage must match control voltage (110 V AC here).

F2
Thermal overload relay

Three bimetal heaters in the motor current. Set the dial to the motor nameplate FLC. NC 95-96 in the coil circuit, NO 97-98 for alarm. Trip class 10 for normal starts. Electronic relays also detect phase loss.

T1 · F3 F4 F5
Control transformer + fuses

Steps 440 V down to 110 V so pushbuttons and lamps are at a safer voltage. It also gives the starter its own undervoltage release. F3/F4 protect the primary, F5 protects the control circuit.

S1 · S2
STOP (NC) & START (NO) pushbuttons

Stops are NC and in series: any one opens the circuit. Starts are NO and in parallel: any one closes it. This is how you add extra local/remote stations.

S0
Remote emergency stop

Latching mushroom or break-glass unit located outside the space (for fuel-oil pumps, purifiers, ventilation fans). Wired as a loop (3 out, 4 back), so a broken cable stops the motor: fail-safe.

H1 H2 H3
Indicator lamps

Green = running, white = stopped/ready, amber = tripped. Colour meanings vary between yards, so follow the vessel’s drawing legend. LED cluster lamps are common now.

X1
Terminal strip

Every wire that leaves the panel (motor, door, remote stations, AMS) lands here. Numbered to match the ferrules. This is where you take measurements and isolate field cables.

CT · P1
Current transformer + ammeter

Lets the engineer watch the load (e.g. pump current rising = clogged strainer or bearing problem). Never open the CT secondary circuit under load: dangerous high voltage.

R1 (option)
Anti-condensation heater

Motors in damp engine rooms and on deck have a space heater. It is switched on via a KM1 NC contact so it heats only while the motor is stopped. It is normally fed from a separate 220 V circuit.

06Forward / Reverse starter: simulation

Used for the windlass, mooring winches, cranes, hatch covers and provision lifts. KM2 swaps L1 and L3 to reverse the motor. If both contactors close together you get a phase-to-phase short circuit, so there are three interlocks: electrical (KM2 21-22 in KM1’s coil circuit and vice versa), pushbutton (each direction button’s NC contact breaks the other rung) and mechanical (a lever between the contactors). Try pressing REV while running forward and watch the current meter. That is “plugging”.

07Star-Delta starter: simulation

Used for large pumps: fire, ballast, sea-water cooling, cargo/stripping. The motor starts in star (KM1 + KM2), so each winding gets 440/√3 ≈ 254 V. That cuts starting current and torque to about ⅓ of DOL and the generators don’t see a big voltage dip. When timer KT runs out, KM2 opens and KM3 closes for delta. F2 carries winding current, so set it to FLC × 0.58. Try setting KT too short (1 s) and watch the current peak at changeover.

08Duty / Standby pump auto-changeover: simulation

Every essential pump pair on board works this way: LO, jacket FW, SW cooling, fuel supply, steering hydraulics. No.1 runs as duty. If discharge pressure falls below the pressure switch setting (here 2.5 bar) for longer than the KT delay (3 s), the standby No.2 starts automatically, latches in and raises an alarm. 09Which starting method? (recommendations)

A ship’s generators have limited capacity, so the main reason to avoid DOL on big motors is the voltage dip it causes. That dip can drop out other contactors or even black out the ship. Values below are typical figures; check the maker’s data.

Learn in this order

1) DOL start/stop. 2) Remote/extra stations (stops in series, starts in parallel). 3) Forward/Reverse interlocks. 4) Star-Delta timing. 5) Duty/standby with pressure switch. 6) Sequential restart and preferential trip. 7) Soft starter and VFD parameters.

Recommend
Local / Remote / Auto selector

Most ER starters have a selector: Local (buttons on the starter), Remote (ECR console or AMS) and Auto (pressure/level/temperature switch). The selector contacts route which START contacts are active. Stops normally work in every position.

Recommend
Level / float control

Bilge, sewage, hydrophore and FW pumps start and stop on two level switches (high = start, low = stop). It is the same seal-in idea: HIGH acts as START and LOW acts as STOP.

10What is different on a ship

The starter circuits are the same as ashore. The difference is the system around them, required by SOLAS and the classification society (ABS, DNV, LR, BV, NK, KR and others).

Insulated system
IT (unearthed) distribution

Most 440 V ship systems have an insulated neutral. A first earth fault does not trip anything. It shows on earth lamps or the insulation monitor and raises an alarm. You must find and clear it quickly, because a second earth fault on another phase is a short circuit.

Blackout recovery
Sequential restart

After a blackout every contactor drops out. Essential services (steering gear, lube oil, cooling water, fuel pumps) are then restarted in timed groups so the restored generator is not overloaded by all starting currents at once.

Generator overload
Preferential trip

Non-essential loads (galley, air-con, reefer groups, etc.) are tripped in stages by the preferential-trip relay when a generator is overloaded. This keeps propulsion and essential services running.

SOLAS
Remote stops outside the space

Fuel-oil transfer/service pumps, purifiers and machinery-space ventilation fans must be stoppable from outside the space (fire control station). They are wired as the S0 loop shown above.

SOLAS
Steering gear motors

Steering gear is not stopped by overload. It has short-circuit protection only, plus an overload alarm and phase-failure alarm on the bridge and in the ECR. It is fed by two separate circuits from the main switchboard (one may go via the emergency switchboard).

Duty / standby
Automatic changeover

Paired pumps (LO, FW, SW, fuel): if the running pump’s discharge pressure drops (pressure switch) or it trips, the standby pump starts automatically and an alarm is raised. This adds a Local/Remote/Auto selector and a pressure-switch contact in parallel with START.

Environment
Vibration, salt, humidity

Use marine type-approved cable (IEC 60092 series), crimped bootlace ferrules, and cable glands of the right size. Enclosure IP rating must suit the location: higher in machinery spaces and on open deck than in the ECR. Re-tighten terminals during planned maintenance.

Higher power
HV motors (6.6 kV)

Large vessels often feed bow thrusters, cargo pumps or reefer plant at 6.6 kV with vacuum contactors and protection relays. The control principle (coil, seal-in, stops in series) is the same, but HV work needs HV-authorised personnel and procedures.

12Fault-finding guide

Safety first: work under a permit-to-work. Isolate Q1 and lock it out/tag it out (LOTO). Prove dead with a tester you have checked on a known live source. Remember that a panel can have more than one supply, such as the heater supply or the AMS loop. For insulation tests use a 500 V DC megger on the motor and cable, with electronics disconnected. Compare against the ship’s records. A commonly used minimum is 1 MΩ, but follow the maker’s and class figures.
